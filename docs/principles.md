# Design Principles

## Primitive interface, sophisticated backend

Complexity belongs behind the interface. A sophisticated information system should not require a sophisticated field interaction.

## Tap it. Say it. Show it.

Use the least expensive input that preserves the truth:
- **Tap** for ordinary conditions and actions.
- **Say** when nuance is easier to dictate than enter.
- **Show** when a photograph communicates better than a form.

AI is a translation layer between human observation and structured municipal information. It proposes structure; people retain judgment.

## Witness before task

Public Works is not primarily a list of work orders. It is stewardship of systems, places, and assets that deserve continued attention.

A rhythm establishes what deserves attention. A witness records what condition is actually present. Conditions generate work when work is warranted.

## Reality dispatches the work

Routing should arise from observed conditions, citizen reports, alarms, planned maintenance, and other real events. The system should not require an office dispatcher to manufacture a task for every ordinary intervention.

## Capture once, derive many

Do not ask for information the system already knows or can reliably derive. Identity, timestamp, context, service area, asset relationships, accounting mappings, labor rates, equipment rates, and reporting classifications should be automatic wherever practical.

## Administrative weight follows operational weight

A full trash can does not deserve the same administrative machinery as a hydrant replacement. Conditions may become routed actions; routed actions may become work orders; substantial work may become projects.

## The system remembers

Completed work should not disappear into an archive. Observations, conditions, interventions, costs, and outcomes become the institutional memory attached to the place, system, or asset.

## Tend the Town; generate the data

Workers should not experience their primary job as data collection. The act of tending the Town should naturally generate the operational record.

## Collect reality, not reports

Never collect information merely because a report needs a field. Capture the underlying operational facts from which reports can be derived.

## New controls must earn their existence

Every additional button, required field, category, and workflow step increases field friction. Add one only when it produces information or control that cannot reasonably be derived another way.
